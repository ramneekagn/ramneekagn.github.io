Research directions:

  • Applying and developing Interpretability Methods
  • AI Safety and Alignment
  • Theoretical foundations of AI
  • AI Ethics

What is the nature of intelligence in large language models, and how do they make complex decisions? I want to approach these questions using the tools of interpretability and, with that understanding, build fairness and safety into AI models and prevent concrete harms such as cyberattacks and bias.

Furthermore I want to bridge technical research in interpretability with the practical and social frameworks from AI Ethics. As a tool I think mechanistic interpretability is crucial for AI alignment, as I don’t believe we should deploy systems whose underlying causal mechanisms we do not understand. As models become more capable, auditing them exclusively through black box analysis might become insufficient.

Currently I am working on Natural Language Autoencoders and reliably evaluating Mechanistic Interpretability methods under the supervision of Prof Dr. Muhammad Bilal Zafar.

  1. Jagged Robustness: Uneven Safety Transfer and Emerging Vulnerabilities under LLM Quantization
    Ramneek Agnihotri, Yingte Xu, and Hans Vath
    preprint, 2026
    Abs HTML

    Quantization enables efficient inference in language models through model parameter compression. This work audits the impact of quantization on capability and safety domains including model safeguards, instruction-following, ethics and bias. Through benchmarks, we find that model safeguards and bias can degrade under quantization, while other capabilities such as truthfulness remain more stable in Qwen3-4B. We define this uneven transfer of LLM capabilities under quantization as Jagged Robustness. We find multiple factors driving Jagged Robustness: task complexity, evaluation method and generation coherence. Moreover, we verify the degradation of model safeguards by observing increased compliance with unsafe requests under quantization, and demonstrate universal adversarial suffix attacks that are benign for the full model but jailbreak quantized models. The findings indicate that it is risky to assume that the performance and robustness can transfer evenly across different capability domains from full models to quantized models.
